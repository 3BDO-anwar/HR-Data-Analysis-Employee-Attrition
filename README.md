## 🎯 The Problem

Employee turnover is expensive. Replacing someone costs time, money, and lost knowledge. This project digs into an HR dataset of **1,470 employees** to find out **who is leaving, and why**, so HR can act before it happens.

## 🔍 What I Analyzed

- 📉 **Attrition rate** overall and by department
- 👤 **Age groups**: which ages leave the most
- 😊 **Job satisfaction** and its link to leaving
- ⚖️ **Work-life balance** impact on attrition
- 📅 **Years in company**: how long people stay before leaving
- 💰 **Monthly rate groups** vs. attrition

## 💡 Key Insights

- **Young employees leave the most:** the 18-25 group has a **36%** attrition rate, almost double the next group (19%).
- **Sales has the highest attrition by department:** **21%**, compared to 14% in Research & Development.
- **Poor work-life balance is a red flag:** employees who rated it 1 leave at **31%**, versus 14% for those who rated it 3.
- **Satisfaction matters:** the lowest satisfaction score (1) goes with **23%** attrition, while the highest (4) drops to **11%**.
- **The first years are the riskiest:** employees with 0-4 years in the company leave at **24%**, falling to 7% for long-tenured staff.

### ✅ Recommendations
- Build onboarding and mentoring programs focused on employees under 26
- Review workload and flexibility in the Sales department
- Track work-life balance and satisfaction scores as early-warning signals
